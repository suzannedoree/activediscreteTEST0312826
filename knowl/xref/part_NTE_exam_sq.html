Item (l).

The sequence \(x\) begins \(0, 3, 8, 15, 24, 35, \ldots\text{.}\)
Solution.
Let’s make a table comparing \(n\) and the terms of the sequence starting at \(n=1\) in factored form, in Table 6.5.18. Notice the first term in the product is one less than \(n\) and the second term in the product is one more than \(n\text{.}\) (We could have factored 0 in many ways, but writing \(0 = 0(2)\) continues the pattern.) A possible formula is \(x_n = (n-1)(n+1)\) for \(n \ge 0\text{.}\)
🔗
Note that \((n+1)(n-1) = n^2-1\) and so there is a quicker way to have found this pattern, provided we thought to compare the terms to \(n^2\) instead, as in Table 6.5.19.
