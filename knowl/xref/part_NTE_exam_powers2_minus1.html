Item (g).

The sequence \(s\) begins \(1, 3, 7, 15, 31, \ldots\text{.}\)
Solution.
To get from each term to the next we add one, add two, then add four, then add eight, so perhaps the pattern involves powers of two. Let’s make a table comparing \(n\text{,}\) \(2^n\text{,}\) and the terms of the sequence starting at \(n=0\text{,}\) in Table 6.5.13. The powers of two and the terms differ by one, but they are not aligned. Let’s write each term using powers of two. We see the power of two is one larger than the index, so the pattern involves \(2^{n+1}\text{.}\) A possible formula is \(s_n = 2^{n+1}-1\) for \(n \ge 0\text{.}\)
