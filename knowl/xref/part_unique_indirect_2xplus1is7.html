Item (c).

Copy the following indirect proof. For a fixed integer \(a\text{,}\) prove that the equation \(2x+1 = a\) has at most one integer solution.
🔗

Proof.
Assume that the integers \(n_1\) and \(n_2\) are each a solution of \(2x+1=a\text{.}\) Then
\begin{equation*} 2n_1+1=a \text{ and } 2n_2+1=a. \end{equation*}
Equating these expressions we get
\begin{equation*} 2n_1+1 = 2n_2+1. \end{equation*}
Subtract one from each side of the equation to get
\begin{equation*} 2n_1 = 2n_2. \end{equation*}
Divide each side of the equation by two to get \(n_1 = n_2\text{.}\) Therefore, the equation \(2x+1=a\) has at most one integer solution.
