Example 4.5.7. Showing two integers are coprime using their prime factors.

 
  1. Explain why distinct primes \(p\) and \(q\) are coprime.
    Solution.
    The positive divisors of \(p\) are 1 and \(p\text{,}\) while the positive divisors of \(q\) are 1 and \(q\text{.}\) Since \(p \neq q\text{,}\) their only common positive divisor is 1 and so \(\fn{gcd}(p,q)=1\text{.}\)

  2. Explain why the integers \(p^a\) and \(q^b\) are coprime when \(p\) and \(q\) are distinct primes.
    Solution.
    The positive divisors of \(p^a\) are 1, \(p\text{,}\) \(p^2\text{,}\) … \(p^a\) while the positive divisors of \(q^b\) are 1, \(q\text{,}\) \(q^2\text{,}\) …, \(q^b\text{.}\) Since \(p \neq q\) are primes, it follows from the uniqueness of prime factorization Theorem 4.4.9 that the only common positive divisor of \(p\) and \(q\) is 1 and so \(\fn{gcd}(p^a,q^b)=1\text{.}\)

  3. Explain why if \(n\) is odd, then 16 and \(n\) are coprime.
    Solution.
    The divisors of 16 are \(\pm1\text{,}\) \(\pm2\text{,}\) \(\pm4\text{,}\) \(\pm8\text{,}\) and \(\pm16\text{.}\) Since \(n\) is odd, we know \(2 \nmid n\text{.}\) By Theorem 4.4.3 (a), it follows that no even integer divides \(n\text{.}\) All divisors of 16 are even, except for \(\pm1\text{.}\) Therefore, the only common divisors of 16 and \(n\) are \(\pm1\) and so \(\fn{gcd}(16,n)=1\text{.}\)

  4. Explain why if \(3 \nmid n\) and \(5 \nmid n\text{,}\) then 15 and \(n\) are coprime.
    Solution.
    The only positive divisors of 15 are 1, 3, 5, and 15. Since \(3 \nmid n\) and \(5 \nmid n\text{,}\) it follows that also \(15 \nmid n\) and so the only common positive divisor of 15 and \(n\) is 1.
