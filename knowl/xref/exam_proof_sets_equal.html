Example 7.2.8. Proof two sets are equal.

Let \(S = \{ b \in \mathbb{B}_5 \mid b \text{ has exactly two \bs{0}s } \}\) and let \(T = \{ b \in \mathbb{B}_5 \mid b \text{ has exactly three \bs{1}s } \}\) where \(\mathbb{B}_5\) is the set of bit strings of length five. For example, \(\bs{10101} \in S\) because it has two \(\bs{0}\) s and \(\bs{10101} \in T\) because it has three \(\bs{1}\) s. Can you see that \(S = T\text{?}\) Prove \(S=T\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
Proof: First, let \(b \in S\text{.}\) That is, \(b\) is a bit string of length five that has exactly two \(\bs{0}\) s. The remaining \(5-2=3\) bits must be \(\bs{1}\) s. Thus \(b \in T\text{.}\)
🔗
Conversely, let \(b \in T\text{.}\) That is, \(b\) is a bit string of length five that has exactly three \(\bs{1}\) s. The remaining \(5-3=2\) bits must be \(\bs{0}\) s. Thus \(b \in S\text{.}\)
