Example 8.3.2. Pattern in matrix powers.

Consider the matrix \(A = \left[ \begin{array}{cc} 1 \amp 1\\ 0 \amp 1 \end{array} \right]\text{.}\)
  1. Calculate \(A^2\) and \(A^3\text{.}\)
    Solution.
    We have
    \begin{equation*} A^2 = \left[ \begin{array}{cc} 1 \amp 1\\ 0 \amp 1 \end{array} \right] \left[ \begin{array}{cc} 1 \amp 1\\ 0 \amp 1 \end{array} \right] = \left[ \begin{array}{cc} 1+0 \amp 1+1\\ 0+0 \amp 0+1 \end{array} \right] = \left[ \begin{array}{cc} 1 \amp 2\\ 0 \amp 1 \end{array} \right]\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Next,
    \begin{equation*} A^3 = AA^2= \left[ \begin{array}{cc} 1 \amp 1\\ 0 \amp 1 \end{array} \right] \left[ \begin{array}{cc} 1 \amp 2\\ 0 \amp 1 \end{array} \right] = \left[ \begin{array}{cc} 1+0 \amp 2+1\\ 0+0 \amp 0+1 \end{array} \right] = \left[ \begin{array}{cc} 1 \amp 3\\ 0 \amp 1 \end{array} \right] \end{equation*}

  2. Make a conjecture about an explicit formula for \(A^n\) for \(n \ge 1\text{.}\)
    Solution.
    Based on our examples, we might conjecture that \(A^n=\left[ \begin{array}{cc} 1 \amp n\\ 0 \amp 1 \end{array} \right]\text{.}\)
