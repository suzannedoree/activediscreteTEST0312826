Example 2.4.8. Bit strings of length 100 and weight three.

How many bit strings of length 100 and weight three are there?
🔗
Solution.
Let’s figure out a way to rephrase this problem in terms of subsets. We can build such a bit string by putting a \(\bs{0}\) or \(\bs{1}\) in each of 100 spaces:
\begin{equation*} \underline{\phantom{X}}~\underline{\phantom{X}}~\underline{\phantom{X}}\dots\underline{\phantom{X}}~\underline{\phantom{X}}~\underline{\phantom{X}} \end{equation*}
🔗
Following the hint in Activity 2.4.B, let’s choose which spaces have \(\bs{1}\) s.
🔗
Step 1: Fill in three spaces with \(\bs{1}\text{.}\) We choose a subset of three spaces for \(\bs{1}\) s from the set of 100 spaces. There are \(\binom{100}{3}\) ways to do this step.
🔗
Step 2: Fill in the remaining 97 spaces with \(\bs{0}\text{.}\) There is one way to do this step because the 97 \(\bs{0}\) s go into the remaining 97 spaces. Alternatively, you can think of choosing a subset of 97 spaces for the \(\bs{0}\) s from the set of 97 remaining spaces. There are \(\binom{97}{97} =1\) ways to do this step.
🔗
Since steps multiply Theorem 2.2.2, there are \(\binom{100}{3}(1) = \binom{100}{3}\) bit strings of length 100 and weight three. Our final answer is \(\binom{100}{3}\text{,}\) which a quick internet search shows equals 161,700 , too many to list by hand!
