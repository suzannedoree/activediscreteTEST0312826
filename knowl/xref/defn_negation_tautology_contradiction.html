Definition 5.1.13. Negation, tautology, and contradiction.

 
  1. The statements \(S\) and \(T\) are negations if \(S\) and \(T\) always have the opposite truth value. That is, \(S\) is true and \(T\) is false or \(S\) is false and \(T\) is true. The statement \(T\) is the negation of \(S\text{,}\) denoted \(\neg S\text{.}\) For example, if \(n\) is an integer and if \(S\) is the statement “\(n >2\)”, then \(\neg S\) is the statement “\(n \le 2\)”.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. A statement \(S\) is a tautology if it is always true. For example, the statement “either I will work on my homework Tuesday night or I will not work on my homework Tuesday night” is a tautology.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. A statement \(S\) is a contradiction if it is always false. For example, the statement “there is an integer \(n\) with \(n>2\) and \(n^2\lt 4\)” is a contradiction because for an integer \(n\) with \(n>2\) we know that \(n^2 > 4\text{.}\)
