The Hamming distance between two bit strings is the number of places where their bits differ. For example, the Hamming distance between 10011 and 11010 is two because they have the same first bits (both 1), different second bits, the same third bits (both 0), the same fourth bits (both 1), and different fifth bits. Since the bit strings differ in two places (second and fifth), their Hamming distance equals two. Hamming distance is used in error-correcting codes to ensure the correct transmission of messages such as text messages.
