Activity 7.5.1. Properties of relations.

For parts Item 1-Item 4, consider the relations on \(X = \{1,2,3\}\) defined by \(T = \{(1,1),(1,2), (2,2), (2,3),(3,1),(3,3)\}\) and \(V = \{(1,1), (2,3), (3,2)\}\text{.}\) Figure 7.5.5 shows their directed graph.
🔗
  1. Give a counterexample to show that \(V\) is not reflexive. Is \(T\) reflexive? Explain.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Give a counterexample to show that \(T\) is not symmetric. Is \(V\) symmetric? Explain.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Give a counterexample to show that \(T\) is not transitive.
    🔗

  4. Give a counterexample to show that \(V\) is not transitive. Hint: The values of \(x\text{,}\) \(y\text{,}\) and \(z\) do not need to be distinct.
    🔗
    🔗
  5. Consider the relation on \(\mathbb{Z}\) defined by
    \begin{equation*} \{(a,b) \mid a, b \in \mathbb{Z} \text{ and } \fn{gcd}(a,b)=1\}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Decide which properties \(R\) has: reflexive, symmetric, transitive. No justification is required if the relation has the property, but provide a counterexample if not.
    🔗
    🔗
  6. Consider the relation \(\sim\) on \(\mathbb{R}^2\) defined by \((x_1,y_1) \sim (x_2,y_2)\) if \(y_2-y_1=3(x_2-x_1)\text{.}\) Check that \(\sim\) is an equivalence relation. Hint: For transitive, note that \((y_3-y_2)+(y_2-y_1)=y_3-y_1\text{.}\)
