Item 1.

Copy the following direct proof. For a fixed integer \(a\text{,}\) prove that the equation \(2x+1 = a\) has at most one integer solution. Proof. Assume \(2x+1 = a\text{.}\) Subtract one from each side of the equation to get \(2x = a-1\text{.}\) Divide each side of the equation by two to get \(x = \frac{a-1}{2}\text{.}\) Thus, if \(x\) is a solution, then \(x = \frac{a-1}{2}\text{.}\) It follows that \(x = \frac{a-1}{2}\) is the only possible solution. Therefore, the equation \(2x+1=a\) has at most one integer solution. \(\Box\) Note: There will be one solution if \(a\) is odd and \(\frac{a-1}{2}\) is an integer. There will be no solutions if \(a\) is even and \(\frac{a-1}{2}\) is not an integer.
