Example 8.5.13. Fibonacci matrix.

Consider the \(2 \times 2\) matrix \(A= \left[ \begin{array}{cc} 1 \amp 1\\ 1 \amp 0 \end{array} \right]\text{.}\)
  1. Calculate \(A^2\text{,}\) \(A^3\text{,}\) and \(A^4\text{.}\)
    Solution.
    First,
    \begin{equation*} A^2= \left[ \begin{array}{cc} 1 \amp 1\\ 1 \amp 0 \end{array} \right]\left[ \begin{array}{cc} 1 \amp 1\\ 1 \amp 0 \end{array} \right]= \left[ \begin{array}{cc} 2 \amp 1\\ 1 \amp 1 \end{array} \right] \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Next,
    \begin{equation*} A^3= A\cdot A^2=\left[ \begin{array}{cc} 1 \amp 1\\ 1 \amp 0 \end{array} \right]\left[ \begin{array}{cc} 2 \amp 1\\ 1 \amp 1 \end{array} \right]= \left[ \begin{array}{cc} 3 \amp 2\\ 2 \amp 1 \end{array} \right] \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Last,
    \begin{equation*} A^4= A\cdot A^3=\left[ \begin{array}{cc} 1 \amp 1\\ 1 \amp 0 \end{array} \right]\left[ \begin{array}{cc} 3 \amp 2\\ 2 \amp 1 \end{array} \right]= \left[ \begin{array}{cc} 5 \amp 3\\ 3 \amp 2 \end{array} \right] \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Make a conjecture about an explicit formula for \(A^n\text{.}\)
    Solution.
    Notice that each power of \(A\) has Fibonacci numbers as entries. For example,
    \begin{equation*} A^4= \left[ \begin{array}{cc} 5 \amp 3\\ 3 \amp 2 \end{array} \right] = \left[ \begin{array}{cc} F_5 \amp F_4\\ F_4 \amp F_3 \end{array} \right]\text{.} \end{equation*}
    We might reasonably conjecture that
    \begin{equation*} A^n= \left[ \begin{array}{cc} F_{n+1} \amp F_n \\ F_n \amp F_{n-1} \end{array} \right] \end{equation*}
    for \(n \ge 1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Use mathematical induction Proof 8.3.2.1 to prove your conjecture.
    Solution.
    We use Proof 8.3.2.1.
    🔗
    Proof. Induct on \(n\text{.}\) Write \(M_n = \left[ \begin{array}{cc} F_{n+1} \amp F_n \\ F_n \amp F_{n-1} \end{array} \right]\text{.}\)
    🔗
    First, when \(n=1\) we have \(A^1=A = \left[ \begin{array}{cc} 1 \amp 1\\ 1 \amp 0 \end{array} \right]\) and \(M_1 = \left[ \begin{array}{cc} F_{2} \amp F_1 \\ F_1 \amp F_0 \end{array} \right] = \left[ \begin{array}{cc} 1 \amp 1 \\ 1 \amp 0 \end{array} \right]\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Next, assume
    \begin{equation*} A^{n-1} = M_{n-1} = \left[ \begin{array}{cc} F_n \amp F_{n-1} \\ F_{n-1} \amp F_{n-2} \end{array} \right] \end{equation*}
    for some integer \(n > 1\text{.}\) Then
    \begin{align*} A^n \amp = AA^{n-1}\\ \amp = \left[ \begin{array}{cc} 1 \amp 1\\ 1 \amp 0 \end{array} \right]\left[ \begin{array}{cc} F_n \amp F_{n-1}\\ F_{n-1} \amp F_{n-2} \end{array} \right]\\ \amp = \left[ \begin{array}{cc} F_n+F_{n-1} \amp F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}\\ F_n \amp F_{n-1} \end{array} \right]\\ \amp = \left[ \begin{array}{cc} F_{n+1} \amp F_n\\ F_n \amp F_{n-1} \end{array} \right]\\ \amp =M_n\text{.} \end{align*}
    🔗
