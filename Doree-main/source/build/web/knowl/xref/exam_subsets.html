  2. How many 3-element subsets does \(A = \{1,2,3,4,5\}\) have?
    Solution.
    There are \(\binom{5}{3}\) 3-element subsets from a set of 5 elements, by definition of the binomial coefficients Definition 2.3.4. We can evaluate \(\binom{5}{3}=10\text{,}\) or just leave the answer as \(\binom{5}{3}\text{.}\)

  3. How many subsets does \(A = \{1,2,3,4,5\}\) have?
    Solution.
    The phrase “how many” indicates that we are counting. Let’s think about how to select a subset \(S\) of \(A\text{.}\) Step 1: decide if \(1 \in S\text{.}\) There are 2 choices: yes or no. (2 ways) Step 2: decide if \(2 \in S\text{.}\) There are again 2 choices: yes or no. (2 ways) Step 3: decide if \(3 \in S\text{.}\) (2 ways). Step 4: decide if \(4 \in S\text{.}\) (2 ways). Step 5: decide if \(5 \in S\text{.}\) (2 ways). Since steps multiply Theorem 2.2.2, there are \(2\cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 2^5\) subsets.
