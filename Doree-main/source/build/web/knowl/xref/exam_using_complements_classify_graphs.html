Example 2.5.18. Using complements to classify graphs.

 
  1. Classify all graphs with four vertices and two edges.
    Solution.
    If the two edges share a common endpoint, then we get a graph \(A\) that has the path \(P_3\) and an isolated fourth vertex. Otherwise, we get a graph \(B\) that has two separate copies of \(P_2\text{.}\) Both are drawn in Figure 2.5.19.

  2. Use the result of (a) to classify all graphs with four vertices and four edges.
    Solution.
    Since the complete graph \(K_4\) has six edges, the complement of any graph with four vertices and four edges must have four vertices and two edges. That is, the complement must be one of the two graphs we found in (a). The two graphs with four vertices and four edges are, therefore, \(\overline{A}\) and \(\overline{B}\) which are also drawn in Figure 2.5.19. We drew them without edge crossings just for fun, so look closely at the vertex labels.
