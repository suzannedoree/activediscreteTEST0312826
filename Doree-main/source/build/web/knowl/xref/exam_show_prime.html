Example 1.4.2. Show an integer is prime or composite.

 
  1. Show that the integer seven is prime.
    Solution.
    We can check that \(2 \nmid 7\) (for example, by calculating \(7 \div 2 = 3.5\) which is not an integer), \(3 \nmid 7\text{,}\) \(4 \nmid 7\text{,}\) \(5 \nmid 7\text{,}\) and \(6 \nmid 7\text{.}\) Therefore, the only positive divisors of seven are one and seven. Thus, seven is prime.

  2. Show that the integer 99 is composite.
    Solution.
    We could list all the divisors of 99, but that is not necessary. We just need one nontrivial positive divisor of 99. For example, \(99 = 9(11)\) and so \(9 \mid 99\text{.}\) That is, 9 is a nontrivial positive divisor of 99. Thus, 99 is composite.
