Activity 9.5.C. Special sums.

 
  1. Consider a real number \(r \neq 1\) and the sum
    \begin{equation*} S = \sum_{k=0}^4r^k = 1 + r + r^2 + r^3 + r^4\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Note that \(S\) is a function of \(r\text{.}\) Evaluate \(S\) when \(r = 10\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Expand \((r-1)S\) and simplify your answer.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Use your answer to Item 2 to find a formula for \(S\) in terms of \(r\text{.}\) Hint: Since \(r \neq 1\text{,}\) you may divide both sides of your equation by \(r-1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Use your formula from Item 3 to evaluate \(S\) when \(r=10\text{.}\) Check that you get the same answer as in Item 1.
    🔗
    🔗
  5. Conjecture an explicit formula for \(\displaystyle \sum_{k=0}^nr^k = 1 + r + r^2 + \ldots + r^n\) when \(r \neq 1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  6. Use your formula from Item 5 to show that \(\displaystyle \sum_{k=0}^n2^k = 2^{n+1}-1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  7. In the Handshakes puzzle Activity 1.1.A, we saw a formula for the sum of the first \(n\) integers, namely Theorem 1.1.3, which says
    \begin{equation*} \sum_{k=1}^nk=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Use this formula to evaluate \(\displaystyle \sum_{k=1}^{10}k\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  8. There is an explicit formula for the sum of the first \(n\) integers squared, namely
    \begin{equation*} \sum_{k=1}^nk^2=\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Use this formula to evaluate \(\displaystyle \sum_{k=1}^{5}k^2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  9. Check your answer to Item 8 by evaluating \(\displaystyle \sum_{k=1}^{5}k^2\) directly.
    🔗
    🔗
  10. Conjecture a formula for \(\displaystyle \sum_{k=1}^nk^3\) by considering the first few partial sums. Hint: Each partial sum is a square. What is the pattern in the integers being squared?
