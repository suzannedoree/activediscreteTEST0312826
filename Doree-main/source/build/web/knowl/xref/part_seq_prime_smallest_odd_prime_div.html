Item (d).

Write a simple explicit rule in words that generates the sequence
\begin{equation*} 1, 1, 3, 1, 5, 3, 7, 1, 3, 5, 11, 3, 13, 7, 3, 1, 17, 3, 19, 5, 3 \ldots \end{equation*}
Start the index at 1. Hint: the rule involves the prime factorization of the index.
Solution.
It can be helpful to look at the terms listed with their index as in Table 6.5.22. Notice that the terms of the sequence \(a_n\) are either 1 or an odd prime. The terms equal to 1 are \(a_1\text{,}\) \(a_2\text{,}\) \(a_4\text{,}\) \(a_8\text{,}\) and \(a_{16}\text{,}\) namely where the index is a power of 2, which no odd prime divides. The terms equal to 3 are \(a_3\text{,}\) \(a_6\text{,}\) \(a_9\text{,}\) \(a_{12}\text{,}\) and \(a_{15}\text{,}\) namely where the index is multiple of 3. The terms equal to 5 are \(a_5\text{,}\) \(a_{10}\text{,}\) and \(a_{20}\) where the index is a multiple of 5, although \(a_{15} \neq 5\text{.}\) The terms equal to 7 are \(a_7\) and \(a_{14}\) where the index is a multiple of 7, although \(a_{21} \neq 7\text{.}\) In each case, the term is an odd prime dividing the index. The last clues are the exceptions \(a_{15}=3\) even though 15 is divisible by both 3 and 5 and \(a_{21}=3\) even though 21 is divisible by both 3 and 7. My guess is that
\begin{equation*} a_n= \left\{ \begin{array}{cc} 1 \amp \text{ if } n \text{ is a power of 2 } \\ \text{ the smallest odd prime divisor of } n \amp \text{ otherwise } \end{array} \right\text{.} \end{equation*}
We use the phrase otherwise to indicate any cases not covered earlier. In this example, those cases are when \(n\) is not a power of 2.
