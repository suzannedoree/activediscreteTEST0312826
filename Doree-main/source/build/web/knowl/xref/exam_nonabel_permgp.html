Example 8.3.11. Nonabelian group.

Let \(\mathbb{S}_3\) be the set of all permutations on the set \(\{1,2,3\}\) with the operation \(\circ\) which is function composition, introduced in Section 6.4. Write
\begin{equation*} \iota = \left( \begin{array}{ccc} 1 \amp 2 \amp 3 \\ 1 \amp 2 \amp 3 \end{array} \right) \sigma_1 = \left( \begin{array}{ccc} 1 \amp 2 \amp 3 \\ 2 \amp 3 \amp 1 \end{array} \right) \sigma_2 = \left( \begin{array}{ccc} 1 \amp 2 \amp 3 \\ 3 \amp 1 \amp 2 \end{array} \right) \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} \tau_1 = \left( \begin{array}{ccc} 1 \amp 2 \amp 3 \\ 1 \amp 3 \amp 2 \end{array} \right) \tau_2 = \left( \begin{array}{ccc} 1 \amp 2 \amp 3 \\ 3 \amp 2 \amp 1 \end{array} \right) \tau_3 = \left( \begin{array}{ccc} 1 \amp 2 \amp 3 \\ 2 \amp 1 \amp 3 \end{array} \right) \end{equation*}
  1. Write the operation table for \(\mathbb{S}_3 = \{ \iota, \sigma_1, \sigma_2, \tau_1, \tau_2, \tau_3\}\text{.}\)
    Solution.
    We show the operation table in Table 8.3.13. For example,
    \begin{equation*} \tau_1 \circ \sigma_1 = \left( \begin{array}{ccc} 1 \amp 2 \amp 3 \\ 1 \amp 3 \amp 2 \end{array} \right) \circ \left( \begin{array}{ccc} 1 \amp 2 \amp 3 \\ 2 \amp 3 \amp 1 \end{array} \right) = \left( \begin{array}{ccc} 1 \amp 2 \amp 3 \\ 3 \amp 2 \amp 1 \end{array} \right)= \tau_2\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Recall that we can compose permutations using dot diagrams as in Figure 8.3.12.
    🔗
    Be careful as
    \begin{equation*} \sigma_1 \circ \tau_1 = \left( \begin{array}{ccc} 1 \amp 2 \amp 3 \\ 2 \amp 3 \amp 1 \end{array} \right) \circ \left( \begin{array}{ccc} 1 \amp 2 \amp 3 \\ 1 \amp 3 \amp 2 \end{array} \right) = \left( \begin{array}{ccc} 1 \amp 2 \amp 3 \\ 2 \amp 1 \amp 3 \end{array} \right)= \tau_3\text{.} \end{equation*}

  2. Check that \(\mathbb{S}_3\) is a group under function composition.
    Solution.
    First, every entry in table is \(\iota\text{,}\) \(\sigma_1\text{,}\) \(\sigma_2\text{,}\) \(\tau_1\text{,}\) \(\tau_2\text{,}\) or \(\tau_3\) so \(\mathbb{S}_3\) is closed under \(\circ\text{.}\) Next, \(\circ\) is associative because
    \begin{equation*} (\alpha \circ (\beta \circ \gamma)) (n) = \alpha((\beta \circ \gamma)(n)) = \alpha(\beta(\gamma(n))) = (\alpha \circ \beta) (\gamma(n)) = ((\alpha \circ \beta ) \circ \gamma) (n) \end{equation*}
    for \(n=1,2,3\text{.}\) Thus \(\alpha \circ (\beta \circ \gamma)= (\alpha \circ \beta ) \circ \gamma\text{.}\) The identity element is \(\iota\text{,}\) as you can check in the table. Note that the inverse of \(\iota\) is \(\iota\text{,}\) the inverse of \(\sigma_1\) is \(\sigma_2\text{,}\) the inverse of \(\sigma_2\) is \(\sigma_1\text{,}\) the inverse of \(\tau_1\) is \(\tau_1\text{,}\) the inverse of \(\tau_2\) is \(\tau_2\text{,}\) and the inverse of \(\tau_3\) is \(\tau_3\text{.}\) Sometimes we say the identity function or the inverse function to emphasis the operation is composition.
