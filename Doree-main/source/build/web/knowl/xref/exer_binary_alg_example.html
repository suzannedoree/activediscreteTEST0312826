Here’s an alternative way to find the binary representation of the integer 100.
  • 100 div 2 = 50 and 100 mod 2 = 0.
    🔗
    🔗
  • 50 div 2 = 25 and 50 mod 2 = 0.
    🔗
    🔗
  • 25 div 2 = 12 and 25 mod 2 = 1.
    🔗
    🔗
  • 12 div 2 = 6 and 12 mod 2 = 0.
    🔗
    🔗
  • 6 div 2 = 3 and 6 mod 2 = 0.
    🔗
    🔗
  • 3 div 2 = 1 and 3 mod 2 = 1.
    🔗
    🔗
  • 1 div 2 = 0 [STOP] and 1 mod 2 = 1.
    🔗
    🔗
  • The binary representation of 100 = \((1100100)_2\text{.}\) Note that we are reading the remainder (mod) from bottom to top.
