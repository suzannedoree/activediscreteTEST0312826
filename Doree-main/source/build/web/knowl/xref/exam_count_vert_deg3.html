Example 6.2.7. Count vertices of degree three.

Let \(\mathbb{G}_{10}\) denote the set of all graphs with ten vertices. The function \(\fn{deg3}: \mathbb{G}_{10} \to \mathbb{N}\) is defined by \(\fn{deg3}(G) =\) the number of vertices of \(G\) that have degree 3.
  1. Identify the domain and codomain of \(\fn{deg3}\text{.}\)
    Solution.
    The domain is \(\mathbb{G}_{10}\) and the codomain is \(\mathbb{N}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Evaluate \(\fn{deg3}(K_{3,7})\) where \(K_{3,7}\) is the complete bipartite graph defined in Definition 3.2.11.
    Solution.
    The complete bipartite graph \(K_{3,7}\) has three (black) vertices of degree seven and seven (white) vertices of degree three, so \(\fn{deg3}(K_{3,7})=7\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Evaluate \(\fn{deg3}(C_{10})\) where \(C_{10}\) is the cycle defined in Definition 3.2.11.
    Solution.
    The cycle \(C_{10}\) has ten vertices of degree two and, therefore, no vertices of degree three, so \(\fn{deg3}(C_{10})=0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

  4. Evaluate \(\fn{deg3}(W_9)\) where \(W_9\) is the wheel defined in Example 3.2.23.
    Solution.
    The wheel \(W_9\) has one hub vertex of degree nine and nine outer vertices of degree three, so \(\fn{deg3}(W_9)=9\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

  5. Evaluate \(\fn{deg3}(L_{2 \times 5})\) where \(L_{2 \times 5}\) is the ladder defined in Example 3.2.23.
    Solution.
    The ladder \(L_{2 \times 5}\) has four corner vertices of degree two and the other \(10-4=6\) inner vertices have degree three, so \(\fn{deg3}(L_{2 \times 5})=6\text{.}\)
