Example 7.5.2. Properties of Relations on a Finite Set.

Consider the relation \(R\) whose directed graph is drawn in Figure 7.5.3.
🔗
  1. Is \(R\) reflexive? Justify your answer.
    Solution.
    No, while there is a loop at 1, there are no loops at 2 or 3. Thus \((1,1)\in R\text{,}\) but \((2,2)\notin R\) and \((3,3) \notin R\text{.}\) To be reflexive, we’d need a loop at every point.

  2. Is \(R\) symmetric? Justify your answer.
    Solution.
    No, we have single-headed arrows. For example, there is an arrow from 2 to 1 but no arrow from 1 to 2. Thus \((1,2) \in R\text{,}\) but \((2,1) \in R\text{.}\) To be symmetric, we’d need only double-headed arrows and loops.

  3. Is \(R\) transitive? Justify your answer.
    Solution.
    Yes. We need to check if \((x,y) \in R\) and \((y,z) \in R\text{,}\) then \((x,z) \in R\text{.}\) It turns out that there are four \((x,y)\text{,}\) \((y,z)\) pairs to check as shown in Table 7.5.4. Since for all four pairs \((x,z) \in R\text{,}\) the relation is transitive.
