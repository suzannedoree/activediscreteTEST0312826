Activity 9.4.D. Explicit formula for binomial coefficients.

Suppose there are 100 movies and we want to rank our top ten as movie #1, movie #2, …, movie #10.
  1. One option is to choose movie #1, and then choose movie #2, and so on. How many ways are there to rank the movies this way? It is okay to have … in your answer.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Rewrite your answer as a fraction where the numerator is \(100!\) and the denominator is another factorial.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. An alternative way to pick your top ten movies would be to first select the ten movies you like best (Step 1), and then to order them from #1 to #10 (Step 2). How many ways are there to rank the movies this way?
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Since we are counting the same quantity, the answers you found in Item 2 and Item 3 must be equal. Write that equation here.
    🔗
    🔗
  5. Solve your equation for \(\binom{100}{10}\text{.}\) What formula did we just prove?
    🔗
    🔗
  6. Conjecture the explicit formula for \(\binom{n}{k}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  7. Test your formula by using it to check by hand that \(\binom{7}{4} = 35\text{,}\) as we saw in Table 9.4.2.
