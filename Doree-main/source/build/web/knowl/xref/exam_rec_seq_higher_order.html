Example 8.5.6. Evaluating higher order recursively-defined sequences.

Write the first five terms of each sequence. Your list should begin with the initial conditions.
  1. The Lucas sequence is defined by \(L_0=2\text{,}\) \(L_1=1\text{,}\) and \(L_n=L_{n-1}+L_{n-2}\) for \(n\ge 2\text{.}\) The Lucas sequence has the same recursion as the Fibonacci sequence, but because the initial conditions are different we get a different sequence.
    Solution.
    We are told that \(L_0=2\) and \(L_1=1\text{.}\) When \(n=2\text{,}\) the recursion gives us
    \begin{equation*} L_2=L_1+L_0=2+1=3\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Next, when \(n=3\) we get
    \begin{equation*} L_3=L_2+L_1=3+1=4\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Finally, when \(n=4\) we have
    \begin{equation*} L_4=L_3+L_2=4+3=7\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The first five terms of the Lucas sequence are \(2,1,3,5,7\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  2. The sequence \(a_n\) is defined by \(a_1=2\text{,}\) \(a_2=-3\text{,}\) and \(a_n=a_{n-2}+(-1)^{n+1}2\) for \(n\ge 3\text{.}\) Notice that this sequence is second order, even though \(a_{n-1}\) is not part of the recursion.
    Solution.
    We are told that \(a_1=2\) and \(a_2=-3\text{.}\) When \(n=3\text{,}\) the recursion gives us
    \begin{equation*} a_3=a_1+(-1)^{3+1}2=2+2=4\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Next, when \(n=4\) we get
    \begin{equation*} a_4=a_2+(-1)^{4+1}2=-3-2=-5\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Finally, when \(n=5\) we have
    \begin{equation*} a_5=a_3+(-1)^{5+1}2=4+2=6\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The first five terms are \(2,-3,4,-5,6\text{.}\) We saw this sequence in Example 6.5.6Item 3 and found an explicit rule for this sequence is \(a_n=(-1)^n(n+2)\) for \(n \ge 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  3. The tribonacci sequence \(t_n\) is defined by \(t_0=0\text{,}\) \(t_1=1\text{,}\) \(t_2=1\text{,}\) and \(t_n=t_{n-1}+t_{n-2}+t_{n-3}\) for \(n\ge 3\text{.}\)
    Solution.
    We are told that \(t_0=0\text{,}\) \(t_1=1\text{,}\) and \(t_2=1\text{.}\) When \(n=3\) we get
    \begin{equation*} t_3=t_2+t_1+t_0=1+1+2\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Next, when \(n=4\) we have
    \begin{equation*} t_4=t_3+t_2+t_1=2+1+1=4\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The first five terms of this sequence are \(0,1,1,2,4\text{.}\) We saw this sequence in Section 2.1  2 where we were tiling the \(1 \times n\) board using \(1\times 1\) squares, \(1 \times 2\) dominoes, and \(1\times 3\) trominoes.
    🔗
