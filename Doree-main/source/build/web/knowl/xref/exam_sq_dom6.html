Example 2.1.2. Squares and dominoes tiling of a \(1 \times 6\) board.

 
  1. As in Activity 2.1.1, list all the ways to tile a \(1 \times 6\) board with squares and dominoes by first listing the ways that start with a square (\(\bullet\)) and then listing all the ways that start with a domino (\(\boldsymbol{-}\)).
    Solution.
    First, we list the ways that start with a square. After the square, there is effectively a \(1 \times 5\) board to tile. Therefore, we copy all the ways to tile the \(1 \times 5\) board (shown with a square bracket) and put a square in front of each, as shown in Figure 2.1.3.
    🔗
    Next, we list the ways that start with a domino. After the domino, there is effectively a \(1 \times 4\) board to tile. Therefore, we copy all the ways to tile the \(1 \times 4\) board (shown with a square bracket) and put a domino in front of each, as shown in Figure 2.1.4.
    🔗
    Since every tiling of a \(1 \times 6\) board either starts with a square or with a domino, we have listed all ways to tile a \(1 \times 6\) board.

  2. How many ways are there to tile a \(1 \times 6\) board? Explain how your answer related to the number of ways to tile a \(1 \times 5\) board and the number of ways to tile a \(1 \times 4\) board.
    Solution.
    We listed a total of 13 ways to tile a \(1 \times 6\) board. Because we get one tiling of a \(1 \times 6\) board for each of the eight ways to tile a \(1 \times 5\) board and we get one tiling of a \(1 \times 6\) board for each of the five ways to tile a \(1 \times 4\) board, , the number of ways to tile a \(1 \times 6\) board equals the sum of the number of ways to tile a \(1 \times 5\) board plus the number of ways to tile a \(1 \times 4\) board. That is, there are \(8+5=13\) ways to tile a \(1 \times 6\) board.
