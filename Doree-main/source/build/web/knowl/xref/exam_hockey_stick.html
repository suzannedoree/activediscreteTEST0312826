Example 9.4.16. Hockey stick.

 
  1. Use the arithmetic triangle to evaluate \(\displaystyle \sum_{k=2}^5 \binom{k}{2}\text{.}\)
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} \sum_{k=2}^5 \binom{k}{2} = \binom{2}{2} + \binom{3}{2} + \binom{4}{2} + \binom{5}{2} = 1 + 3 + 6 + 10 = 20 \end{equation*}

  2. Use the arithmetic triangle to evaluate \(\displaystyle \sum_{k=3}^7 \binom{k}{3}\text{.}\)
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} \sum_{k=3}^7 \binom{k}{3} = \binom{3}{3} + \binom{4}{3} + \binom{5}{3} + \binom{6}{3} + \binom{7}{3} = 1 + 4 + 10 + 20 + 35 = 70 \end{equation*}

    🔗

  3. Conjecture an explicit formula for the sum \(\displaystyle \sum_{k=m}^n \binom{k}{m}\text{.}\)
    Solution.
    From (a) we know
    \begin{equation*} \sum_{k=2}^5 \binom{k}{2} = 20 = \binom{6}{3}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    From (b) we know
    \begin{equation*} \sum_{k=3}^7 \binom{k}{3} = 70 = \binom{8}{4}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Based on these two examples, it is reasonable to conjecture that
    \begin{equation*} \sum_{k=m}^n \binom{k}{m} = \binom{n+1}{m+1} \end{equation*}
