Example 4.4.11. Prime factorization of divisors.

 
  1. List the positive divisors of the integer 16. Compare the prime factorization of 16 with the prime factorization of its positive divisors.
    Solution.
    The positive divisors of \(16=2^4\) are \(1, 2, 4, 8\) and \(16\text{.}\) The prime factorizations of these divisors are \(1, 2^1, 2^2, 2^3\text{,}\) and \(2^4\text{.}\) We can display this list in a table. Table 4.4.12 lists the divisors in prime factored form and then the divisors themselves.

  2. List the positive divisors of the integer 50. Compare the prime factorization of 50 with the prime factorization of its positive divisors.
    Solution.
    The positive divisors of \(50=2\cdot 5^2\) are \(1, 2, 5, 10, 25\) and \(50\text{.}\) The prime factorizations of these divisors are \(1, 2^1, 5^1, 2 \cdot 5, 5^2\) and \(2\cdot 5^2\text{.}\) Table 4.4.13 lists the divisors in prime factored form and then the divisors themselves.   3. List the positive divisors of the integer 72. Compare the prime factorization of 72 with the prime factorization of its positive divisors.
    Solution.
    The positive divisors of \(72=2^2 \cdot 3^2\) are \(1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 12, 18, 24, 36\) and \(72\text{.}\) The prime factorizations of these divisors are \(1, 2^1, 3^1, 2^2, 2 \cdot 3, 2^3, 3^2, 2^2 \cdot 3, 2 \cdot 3^2, 2^3 \cdot 3, 2^2 \cdot 3^2\text{,}\) and \(2^2 \cdot 3^2\text{.}\) Table 4.4.14 lists the divisors in prime factored form and then the divisors themselves. Notice that going across a row of this table, we multiply by two and going down a column of this table, we multiply by three.
