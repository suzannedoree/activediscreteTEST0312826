Example 6.4.15. Inverse of a permutation.

Consider the permutation
\begin{equation*} \sigma = \left(\begin{array}{ccc c} 1 \amp 2 \amp 3 \amp 4 \\ 2 \amp 3 \amp 4 \amp 1 \end{array} \right)\text{.} \end{equation*}
Since \(\sigma\) is a permutation, it is one-to-one and onto. Thus, \(\sigma\) is invertible.
  1. Find the inverse \(\sigma^{-1}\text{.}\)
    Solution.
    We display our work in Table 6.4.16. Thus
    \begin{equation*} \sigma^{-1} = \left(\begin{array}{ccc c} 1 \amp 2 \amp 3 \amp 4 \\ 4 \amp 1 \amp 2 \amp 3 \end{array} \right) \end{equation*}

  2. Calculate \(\sigma^{-1} \circ \sigma\) using the dot diagrams.
    Solution.
    We show the dot diagrams in Figure 6.4.17. Thus, \(\sigma^{-1} \circ \sigma = \left(\begin{array}{ccc c} 1 \amp 2 \amp 3 \amp 4 \\ 1 \amp 2 \amp 3 \amp 4 \end{array} \right) = i\text{.}\)
