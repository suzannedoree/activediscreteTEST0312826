Activity 6.4.C. Uniqueness proofs.

 
  1. Copy the following direct proof. For a fixed integer \(a\text{,}\) prove that the equation \(2x+1 = a\) has at most one integer solution. Proof. Assume \(2x+1 = a\text{.}\) Subtract one from each side of the equation to get \(2x = a-1\text{.}\) Divide each side of the equation by two to get \(x = \frac{a-1}{2}\text{.}\) Thus, if \(x\) is a solution, then \(x = \frac{a-1}{2}\text{.}\) It follows that \(x = \frac{a-1}{2}\) is the only possible solution. Therefore, the equation \(2x+1=a\) has at most one integer solution. \(\Box\) Note: There will be one solution if \(a\) is odd and \(\frac{a-1}{2}\) is an integer. There will be no solutions if \(a\) is even and \(\frac{a-1}{2}\) is not an integer.

  3. Copy the following indirect proof. For a fixed integer \(a\text{,}\) prove that the equation \(2x+1 = a\) has at most one integer solution. Proof. Assume that the integers \(n_1\) and \(n_2\) are each a solution of \(2x+1=a\text{.}\) Then \(2n_1+1=a\) and \(2n_2+1=a\text{.}\) Equating these expressions we get \(2n_1+1 = 2n_2+1\text{.}\) Subtract one from each side of the equation to get \(2n_1 = 2n_2\text{.}\) Divide each side of the equation by two to get \(n_1 = n_2\text{.}\) Therefore, the equation \(2x+1=a\) has at most one integer solution. \(\Box\)
