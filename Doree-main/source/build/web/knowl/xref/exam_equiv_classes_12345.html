Example 7.5.10. Equivalence classes for an equivalence relation on \(\{1,2,3,4,5\}\).

Consider the equivalence relation \(R\) on \(X = \{1,2,3,4,5\}\) defined by the directed graph drawn in Figure 7.5.11.
🔗
  1. Calculate \([1]\text{.}\)
    Solution.
    There are three arrows out of 1 or, equivalently, three ordered pairs of the form \((1,\underline{~})\text{,}\) namely \((1,1)\text{,}\) \((1,3)\text{,}\) and \((1,4)\text{.}\) Thus \([1]=\{1,3,4\}\text{.}\)

  2. Calculate \([2]\text{.}\)
    Solution.
    There are two arrows out of 2 or, equivalently, two ordered pairs of the form \((2,\underline{~})\text{,}\) namely \((2,2)\) and \((2,5)\text{.}\) Thus \([2]=\{2,5\}\text{.}\)

  3. How many different equivalence classes are there?
    Solution.
    We can check that \([3]=\{1,3,4\}\text{,}\) \([4]=\{1,3,4\}\text{,}\) and \([5]=\{2,5\}\text{.}\) Therefore, there are only two equivalence classes: \(\{1,3,4\}\) and \(\{2,5\}\text{.}\) It is convenient to refer to these equivalence classes using the smallest possible representative as \([1]\) and \([2]\text{.}\)

  4. Describe the quotient space \(X/R\text{.}\)
    Solution.
    The quotient space consists of two equivalence classes: \(X/R=\{[1],[2]\}\text{.}\)
