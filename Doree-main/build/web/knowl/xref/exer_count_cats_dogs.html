21. Practice.

Suppose there are \(n\) cats and \(n\) dogs and we want to choose \(n\) animals.
  1. If we picked \(k\) cats, then how many dogs do we need to choose?
    🔗
    🔗
  2. How many ways are there to pick \(k\) cats and the rest dogs?
    🔗
    🔗
  3. If we picked one favorite animal, how many ways are there to pick the rest? Hint: how many animals are left and how many more do we need?
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Hint.
  1. \(\displaystyle n-k\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Hint: Step 1 is to pick \(k\) out of \(n\) cats. Step 2 is to pick \(n-k\) out of \(n\) dogs. Steps multiply.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Hint: There are \(n+n=2n\) animals to start, so after we pick a favorite animal there are \(2n-1\) animals remaining.
    🔗
    🔗
