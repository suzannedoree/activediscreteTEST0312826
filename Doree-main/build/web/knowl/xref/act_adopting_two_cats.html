Activity 2.3.A. Adopting two cats.

 
  1. There are five cats (\(\str{c1}\text{,}\) \(\str{c2}\text{,}\) \(\str{c3}\text{,}\) \(\str{c4}\text{,}\) \(\str{c5}\)) waiting to be adopted at the animal shelter. You want to adopt two of the cats. Make a list of possibilities. Notice that the order in which we choose the cats does not matter. For example, choosing \(\str{c2}\) and \(\str{c5}\) is the same as choosing \(\str{c5}\) and \(\str{c2}\text{.}\) For that reason, we use set notation to indicate a choice, such as \(\{\str{c2}, \str{c5}\}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. In how many ways can you choose two cats to adopt?
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Betelehem tried to answer (b) without making a list. Here is her work.
    Solution.
    We will count the possibilities using steps.
    🔗
    Step 1: Choose the first cat. (5 ways)
    🔗
    Step 2: Choose the second cat. Note that the second cat must be a different cat from the first cat. (4 ways)
    🔗
    Since steps multiply Theorem 2.2.2, there must be \(5 \times 4 = 20\) ways to adopt two cats.
    🔗
    🔗
