The Hamming distance between two bit strings is the number of places where their bits differ. For example, the Hamming distance between \(\bs{10011}\) and \(\bs{11010}\) is two because they have the same first bits (both \(\bs{1}\)), different second bits, the same third bits (both \(\bs{0}\)), the same fourth bits (both \(\bs{1}\)), and different fifth bits. Since the bit strings differ in two places (second and fifth), their Hamming distance equals two. Hamming distance is used in error-correcting codes to ensure the correct transmission of messages such as text messages.
