Definition 9.3.1. Partial sums.

Consider a list of numbers \(a_s, a_{s+1}, a_{s+2}, \ldots, a_n\) for some integers \(s\) and \(n\text{.}\)
  1. For the sum
    \begin{equation*} \sum_{k=s}^n a_k =a_s + a_{s+1}+a_{s+2}+\ldots+a_n\text{,} \end{equation*}
    the partial sums are
    \begin{align*} \sum_{k=s}^s a_k \amp =a_s,\\ \sum_{k=s}^{s+1} a_k \amp =a_s+a_{s+1},\\ \sum_{k=s}^{s+2} a_k \amp =a_s+a_{s+1}+a_{s+2}\text{,} \end{align*}
    and so on. For example, for the sum
    \begin{equation*} \sum_{k=1}^n 2 =2 + 2 + 2 + \ldots + 2\text{,} \end{equation*}
    the partial sums are
    \begin{align*} \sum_{k=1}^1 2 \amp =2,\\ \sum_{k=1}^2 2 \amp =2+2=4,\\ \sum_{k=1}^3 2 \amp =2+2+2=6\text{,} \end{align*}
    and so on.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. The sequence of partial sums is
    \begin{equation*} a_s, a_s+a_{s+1},a_s+a_{s+1}+a_{s+2}, \ldots\text{.} \end{equation*}
    For example, the sequence of partial sums in our example begins
    \begin{equation*} 2, 4, 6, \ldots\text{.} \end{equation*}
