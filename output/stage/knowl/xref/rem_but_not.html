Remark 5.1.15. But not.

It is common in English to pronounce \(P \land \neg Q\) as “\(P\) but not \(Q\)”. The word “but” has the same meaning logically as “and” but in English it indicates that what follows is negative in some way. For example, while we can say I like chocolate ice cream and I don’t want any now. it is more common to say I like chocolate ice cream but I don’t want any now.
