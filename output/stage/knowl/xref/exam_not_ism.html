Example 3.5.2. Showing \(G_1\) and \(G_3\) are nonisomorphic.

Although they share many common properties, the graphs \(G_1\) and \(G_3\) drawn in Figure 3.4.1 are not isomorphic. Prove it.
🔗
Solution.
Let’s start with the labeling of \(G_1\) from Figure 3.4.8. The only degree 3 vertex in \(G_1\) is labeled \(c\text{.}\) That means \(c\) is the only vertex that appears in exactly three edges. The graph \(G_3\) has one degree 3 vertex, and if we want to produce the same edge list as \(G_1\text{,}\) that vertex needs to be labeled \(c\) to match, as shown in Figure 3.5.3.
🔗
Next, in \(G_1\text{,}\) the vertex \(c\) has exactly one neighbor with degree one, and it is labeled \(x\text{.}\) But in \(G_3\text{,}\) the vertex \(c\) has two neighbors with degree one. If we label one of those neighbors \(x\text{,}\) the other cannot be labeled (?). We are stuck. There is no common labeling. Therefore, \(G\) and \(K\) are not isomorphic.
🔗
Notice that we have identified a property that distinguishes \(G_1\) from \(G_3\text{,}\) namely \(G_1\) has exactly one vertex with degree three and that vertex has exactly one neighbor with degree one, but \(G_3\) does not have that property. This observation gives us a shorter proof that \(G_1\) and \(G_3\) are not isomorphic. After all, if they were the same graph, then they would have shared all graph-theoretic properties.
