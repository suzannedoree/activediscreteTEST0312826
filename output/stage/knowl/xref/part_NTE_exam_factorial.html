Item (h).

The sequence \(t\) begins \(4, 5, 9, 27, 123, 603, \ldots\text{.}\)
Solution.
The terms are growing large quickly. Let’s look at their ratios \(\frac{5}{4} = 1.25\text{,}\) \(\frac{9}{5} = 1.8\text{,}\) \(\frac{27}{9}=3\text{,}\) \(\frac{123}{27} = 4.55.\text{..,}\) \(\frac{603}{123} = 4.90.\text{...}\) It’s almost like we are multiplying by one, then multiplying by two, then by three, then by four, then by five. That pattern would involve \(1 \times 2 \times \cdots \times n =n!\) Let’s make a table comparing \(n\text{,}\) \(n!\text{,}\) and the terms of the sequence starting at \(n=1\text{,}\) in Table 6.5.14. A possible formula is \(t_n = n!+3\) for \(n \ge 1\text{.}\)
