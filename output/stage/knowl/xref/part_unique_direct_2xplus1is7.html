Item (a).

Copy the following direct proof. For a fixed integer \(a\text{,}\) prove that the equation \(2x+1 = a\) has at most one integer solution.
🔗

Proof.
Assume
\begin{equation*} 2x+1 = a\text{.} \end{equation*}
Subtract one from each side of the equation to get
\begin{equation*} 2x = a-1\text{.} \end{equation*}
Divide each side of the equation by two to get
\begin{equation*} x = \frac{a-1}{2}\text{.} \end{equation*}
🔗
Thus, if \(x\) is a solution, then \(x = \frac{a-1}{2}\text{.}\) It follows that \(x = \frac{a-1}{2}\) is the only possible solution.
🔗
Therefore, the equation \(2x+1=a\) has at most one integer solution, namely \(x = \frac{a-1}{2}\text{.}\)
🔗
Note: There will be one solution if \(a\) is odd and \(\frac{a-1}{2}\) is an integer. There will be no solutions if \(a\) is even and \(\frac{a-1}{2}\) is not an integer.
