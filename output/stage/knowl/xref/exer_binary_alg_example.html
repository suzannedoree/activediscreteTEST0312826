Here’s an alternative way to find the binary representation of the integer 100.
  • 100 \(\fn{div}\) 2 = 50 and 100 \(\fn{mod}\) 2 = 0.

  • 50 \(\fn{div}\) 2 = 25 and 50 \(\fn{mod}\) 2 = 0.

    🔗

  • 3 \(\fn{div}\) 2 = 1 and 3 \(\fn{mod}\) 2 = 1.
    🔗

  • 1 \(\fn{div}\) 2 = 0 [STOP] and 1 \(\fn{mod}\) 2 = 1.
    🔗
    🔗
  • The binary representation of 100 = \((1100100)_2\text{.}\) Note that we are reading the remainder (\(\fn{mod}\)) from bottom to top.
