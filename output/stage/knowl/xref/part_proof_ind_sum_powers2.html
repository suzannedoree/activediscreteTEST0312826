Item (a).

Copy the following proof using mathematical induction to prove that \(\displaystyle \sum_{k=0}^n2^k=2^{n+1}-1\) for \(n \ge 0\text{.}\)

Proof.
Write \(f(n) = 2^{n+1}-1\text{.}\) Induct on \(n\text{.}\)
🔗
First, when \(n=0\) we know that \(\displaystyle \sum_{k=0}^02^k=2^0=1\) and also
\begin{equation*} f(0)=2^{0+1}-1=2-1=1. \end{equation*}
🔗
Next, assume \(\displaystyle \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}2^k=f(n-1)= 2^{(n-1)+1}-1=2^n-1\) for some integer \(n > s\text{.}\)
🔗
Then
\begin{align*} \sum_{k=0}^n2^k \amp = \left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}2^k\right) + 2^n \\ \amp = f(n-1) + 2^n = \left(2^n-1\right)+2^n \\ \amp = 2^n+2^n-1=2\cdot2^n-1=2^{n+1}-1 \\ \amp = f(n)\text{.} \end{align*}
